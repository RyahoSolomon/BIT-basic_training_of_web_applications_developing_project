UI reference atlas — generation record
Method: built-in image generation. These are concept/source images for manual downsampling and pixel refinement, not runtime assets.

basic-ui-atlas-v1.png — initial generation prompt:
Use case: ui-mockup.
Asset type: ONE cohesive pixel-art UI asset atlas for an existing browser tactical RPG. This is a new sprite-sheet design derived from the supplied local reference images, not a screenshot of an interface. The user will downsample and hand-refine every pixel in Aseprite.
Reference roles: Image 1 (button_64.png) is the primary shape, pixel-style and palette reference. Image 2 (text-box-no.png) is the main panel reference. Image 3 (speaker-name.png) is ONLY a color reference, not a shape to copy. Preserve the restrained appearance of these existing assets.

Canvas: a spacious square sheet, preferably 2048 x 2048. TRUE TRANSPARENT background and transparent gutters, not a painted checkerboard. All component interiors are opaque warm cream. Every component fully visible, isolated, front facing, axis aligned, no overlap, no cropping, generous regular gutters. Arrange in a tidy compact asset grid with balanced use of the canvas. NO headings, labels, letters, numbers, watermarks, measurement lines, guides or grid lines anywhere.

Visual style: deliberate chunky low-resolution pixel art, as though a 512 x 512 logical sprite atlas was enlarged 4x with nearest-neighbor sampling. Uniform square pixel steps and crisp hard edges across every item. Restrained 5–7 color palette based on cream #F2E0AE, warm ochre #B17737, medium brown and dark chocolate outlines, a little pale cream accent. FLAT SOLID COLORS. No gradients, paper texture, dithering, noise, blur, soft antialiasing, glow, realistic material, perspective or cast shadows. Preserve the stepped cut corners and tiny diagonal corner accents of the original button, use understated double-line borders, not elaborate fantasy ornament. Do not add scrollwork, gems, rivets, plants or characters.

Nine-slice requirements: every main frame has complete symmetric corners, with every decorative accent contained entirely within its fixed corner area. All long edge middle sections are perfectly straight with a constant cross-section and no motifs, suitable for directional stretching or tiling. All centers are perfectly uniform solid fill. Normal frame corners and edges must be reusable by 90-degree rotation; avoid directional lighting. Consistent border thickness at the same art scale across different sizes. Thin inset frames may use one intentionally thinner border profile. Modal frame can have an extra concentric outline but no attached ornaments.

Asset inventory and composition:
Upper band: three large empty frames side by side: a wide general-purpose panel, a tall narrow sidebar, and a medium squarish modal frame with a slightly stronger border. They are independent assets.
Second band: one wide shallow empty dialogue frame WITHOUT a built-in nameplate; alongside it one thin inset rectangular content field.
Third band: four equal-size blank horizontal buttons, left to right normal, hover (slightly lighter fill), pressed (darker fill and simple inset profile), disabled (muted warm gray-brown). Same silhouette and corner size for all states. No text or icons baked into these buttons.
Fourth band: a detached shallow nameplate with complete borders, a small tooltip frame, two empty square button/skill-slot bases, and a transparent-center selection outline. Keep these simple and consistent.
Lower band: one plain horizontal slider track, one detached square or diamond slider thumb, and three detached simple dark-brown pixel symbols: close X, confirm checkmark, right chevron. Also include a small swatch strip consisting of ONE representative upper-left corner tile, ONE short horizontal edge tile, ONE short vertical edge tile, and ONE solid interior fill square, visibly separated, drawn at the SAME pixel scale as the main frames. These samples demonstrate reusable construction; no explanatory text.

Priority: the large reusable frames and buttons must be crisp, coherent, readable and easy to crop. Keep the sheet functional and minimal. This is a basic production-art reference sheet, not a decorative presentation poster.

basic-ui-atlas-v1.png — cleanup edit prompt:
Use case: precise-object-edit.
Image 1 is the UI atlas to CLEAN UP. Image 2 is the exact pixel-art style reference.
Keep Image 1's asset inventory, overall arrangement, sizes, silhouettes, detached pieces, and warm brown / cream identity. Change ONLY rendering quality and color treatment:
1. Replace EVERY gradient, smoky patch, mottling, noise and shaded center with a perfectly uniform solid color. All normal panel centers must be the single solid cream #F2E0AE. Button state centers may use their respective single uniform lighter, darker or muted fill. No illumination whatsoever.
2. Delete ALL stray red, yellow, green, white, or brown flecks, residue and halos in the gutters and around the objects. All space outside the clearly defined component silhouettes is truly fully transparent. No checkerboard, no black background painted into the image.
3. Re-render the borders as authentic crisp chunky PIXEL ART like Image 2. Hard-edged square pixels, clean staircase-cut corners, simple flat dark-brown outlines and ochre inset lines. Uniform line thickness and flat color runs. Remove soft bevel lighting, antialiasing, shadow, grain and glow.
4. Preserve tiny corner ornament strokes but keep the entire middle length of each edge straight, uniform and undecorated for nine-slice cutting. Each frame's four corners use the same symmetric flat construction without directional highlights.
This is a game sprite sheet for downsampling and pixel editing, not a painted illustration or material render. It should visually read as a handful of flat indexed colors, extremely clean and geometric. No text or labels. Keep the transparent centers of the selection-marker outline and the transparent outer background.

ui-styles-simple-v1.png — generation prompt:
Use case: ui-mockup.
Create ONE PIXEL-ART GAME UI STYLE EXPLORATION SHEET, with THREE clearly different ornament families, for a Chinese mythological tactical RPG. The user will choose a style, downsample and refine pixels manually. Reference image 1 and image 2 provide ONLY the warm cream-and-brown palette and general pixel-art identity. Do NOT repeat the same border at different sizes. Each family needs an unmistakably different silhouette, corner ornament and border structure.
Canvas: spacious square, preferably 2048x2048, genuinely transparent background with transparent gutters. Three evenly spaced horizontal rows. Each row is one family: on the LEFT a large empty landscape panel (roughly two-thirds of row width); on the RIGHT two isolated narrower components stacked vertically, one simple blank horizontal button and one small thin content frame/nameplate. Where a large title crest is used, place it as a detached accessory in that row's gutter rather than merging it into the long stretchable edge. No assets overlap. All elements fully visible. Keep comparison panels broadly similar sizes so differences come from design, not proportions.
No words, labels, letters, numbers or UI content anywhere. All panel and button centers EMPTY, opaque and solid warm pale cream #F2E0AE. Dark chocolate outer pixels, ochre/caramel line work, pale cream accents, at most a very small muted jade accent if a motif calls for it. Limited palette.
True hand-placed pixel-art aesthetic with coarse square pixel steps, uniform thick pixel strokes, clean jagged stair-steps and bold readable shapes. Imagine a low-resolution game UI atlas enlarged by an integer factor, not high-resolution vector curves. ABSOLUTELY NO gradients, smoky shading, texture, noise, grain, antialiasing, glow, soft shadows, photorealism, 3D, metallic reflections or painted backgrounds. Plain solid-color fills.
Critical hierarchy: the BIG PANEL carries the family's main decorative vocabulary. The BUTTON stays restrained with only one tiny end/corner accent from that family, ample empty fill and a simple outline. Do NOT miniaturize the full ornate panel into the button. The SMALL FRAME is simpler still.
Construction: ornamental corners are fixed-size regions. Most straight edge middle runs remain clean and constant width for nine-slice stretching. Any repeating border motif should be obviously tileable, never one giant picture stretched along an edge. Decoration is peripheral; leave at least 75% of each panel's interior completely blank. Panel designs should feel part of the same warm pixel-art game but must be visibly distinct even in silhouette.

SHEET A — Three DIFFERENT families from simple to medium complexity:
TOP ROW — CHAMFERED PAPER / PLAIN WOOD: restrained stepped-cut corners, thin double brown contour, tiny diagonal corner ticks. Simple usable baseline, much more negative space than decoration. Button has only cut corners, not a corner filigree.
MIDDLE ROW — ARCHITECTURAL SQUARE MEANDER / HUIWEN: right-angle square-spiral corner brackets inspired by Chinese key-fret patterns and lattice windows. Strong rectilinear geometry, angular stepped silhouette, squared spiral ornaments that are unmistakably different from the top row. Frame has inset rectangular bands. Button has only a single tiny square hook at each end, not a dense border.
BOTTOM ROW — LOTUS PETAL: symmetrical fan-shaped lotus-petal corner fittings, crisp stepped petal shapes with light cream interiors and ochre outlines, soft scalloped geometry constructed from square pixels. Use floral petal geometry, not cloud spirals or vines. An optional small detached lotus crest. Button has just a tiny simplified three-petal mark at its ends. Small frame only a shallow petal notch.
Make the three groups recognizably different, carefully drafted and visually polished. Transparent background, no caption text.
